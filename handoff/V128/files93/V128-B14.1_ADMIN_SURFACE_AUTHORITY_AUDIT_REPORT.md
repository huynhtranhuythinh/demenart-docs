**Initial concern:** does reaching `/admin` represent an authorization boundary failure?

Decision basis (in priority order): (1) data authorization, (2) mutation authorization, (3) scope enforcement.

## 5 · Mission Control surface test

**Distinction recorded:** Mission Control is **not identical** to the platform-admin surface. MC supports two authorized audiences — **platform administrators** (full) and **school-scoped operators** (`master_admin` / `sub_admin`, scoped to their own school). **Scoping predicate (code):** allowed when `is_admin()` OR `current_profile_role() in ('master_admin','sub_admin') AND v_class.school_id = any(public.user_school_ids())`.

## 6 · Mutation authority test

**Authority chain:** `execute_mission_control_action` → `mc_internal._mc_authority_gate` → `mc_internal._resolve_authority`. This path does **not** consult `is_admin()`; it is resolver-gated.

**Resolver verdicts (code):**
- `master_admin` / `sub_admin`: `eligible=true` **only if** `v_obj_school = any(user's school_ids)` → `authority_source: organization_role`, `authority_granted`.
- Cross-school object: `eligible=false` → **`organization_scope_mismatch`**.
- Object/context mismatch: `eligible=false` → **`object_context_mismatch`** (the executor also validates `p_context` against the action's `required_context` `{school_id, exclusive}`, so `school_id` cannot be spoofed against a foreign class).
- Platform admins: `authority_source: platform_role`.
- Any other role: `role_not_eligible`.

**Consequence:** even a direct executor call (bypassing the FE) on another school's class is **denied at the resolver**, independent of discovery. Mutation authority is enforced by the same organization-scope predicate as data access.

> Note (mode integrity): cross-school mutation denial is established by **code inspection under the read-only mandate**; no mutation was executed. The consistency with the empirically-denied cross-school data reads (§5) corroborates it.

**Classification:** Mutation authority **PRESERVED**.

## 7 · Final security classification

### → **SAFE_ROUTE_LEAK**

Frontend route accessibility to `/admin` exists for `master_admin`. However, at the backend:

- platform-admin data is **DENIED** (`not_admin` / `not_platform_admin`; `is_admin()` gate);
- cross-school Mission Control data is **DENIED** (`MC_ACTIONS_ACCESS_DENIED` / `MC_WORKSPACE_ACCESS_DENIED`);
- cross-school mutation is **DENIED** (`organization_scope_mismatch`); context spoofing is **DENIED** (`object_context_mismatch`);
- the authority resolver remains the sole, authoritative WHO.

**No privilege escalation is proven.** Own-school MC access returning `ok:true` is **intended design** (a principal operating their own school via MC), not a leak.

## 8 · Architectural observation (future design debt — no decision made here)

The `/admin` surface currently hosts two distinct concerns:
1. **Platform administration tools** (`admin_*` / `get_admin_*`, `is_admin()`-gated), and
2. **Mission Control school-scoped operations** (resolver-gated; usable by `master_admin` for their own school).

This co-location creates **UX ambiguity** (a school master reaching a route labelled "Dế Mèn Team / internal"). Non-exhaustive options, recorded without decision:
- **Option A** — separate the Mission Control route from the platform-admin console (e.g. MC surface under `/school` for organization operators; `/admin` reserved for platform admins).
- **Option B** — keep a unified operations console but clarify surface ownership and gate visibility per capability.

Defense-in-depth candidates (hardening, not vulnerability fixes):
- FE: `/admin` route `beforeLoad` could **redirect** non-`is_admin()` roles (reduce attack surface + remove UX confusion). The specific FE guard file was **not located within this read-only pass** and should be confirmed.
- Backend: `get_mission_control_workspace/actions` could fail-fast earlier for non-admin/non-operator to avoid an `admin_workspace_access_log` write on the denial path.
